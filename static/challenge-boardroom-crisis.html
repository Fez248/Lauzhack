Playbook

Six rules cover most of a first day. Cat keeps the facts straight; Turtle keeps the right people in the right order. Tap a card to read it up close. The decisions will point you back here.

🐱 CatFacts before moves

Contain, then think

A panicking board makes the attacker's job easy. Do the boring things first, in the right order, and keep the evidence.

  1. Cut the cable, keep the power.Disconnect affected machines from the network, but don't switch them off or wipe them. The evidence lives in memory and on disk.
  2. Assume they are reading your mail.Move the crisis team to phones and a separate chat until you know the attacker is out.
  3. Say only what you can prove."No data was affected" is a promise. Break it once and nobody believes your next update.

Calm isn't a feeling. It's an order of operations.

🐢 TurtleRight people, right order

Nobody does this alone

I have seen many storms, dear. The boards that came through them well were never the cleverest. They simply called the right people early and told the truth.

  1. Call the experts before you decide anything big.Legal, your insurer, the police and an incident response team come first. That includes the ransom: paying guarantees nothing and funds the next attack.
  2. Tell the right people, on time.A regulator's clock starts when you become aware: 72 hours under GDPR, "as soon as possible" in Switzerland. Customers need clear steps before the attacker leaks the data.
  3. Fix the door, not the person.Reset credentials, add multi-factor authentication, restore clean. Punishing whoever clicked teaches everyone to hide the next mistake.

A team that stays calm in the storm was looked after long before it.
